Storage layout. In our design, a level below the top can
hold three tables at once. Its previous outgoing table stays
queryable until the next level absorbs it, and its partial table
stays queryable while pre-routing builds the replacement in
separate storage. Three full-size regions are not necessary,
however, because a partial table holds at most B −1 carries.
Each level therefore reserves two regions of BUℓbuckets
and one region of (B −1)Uℓbuckets (Figure 5). The partial
table and its replacement alternate between the smaller region
and one full-size region, timed so that the last merge of the
cycle writes into the full-size region. The full table is then
already where the next outgoing table belongs, so it needs
no relocation copy. Once the next level retires the previous
outgoing table P, its region pairs with the smaller one for the
next cycle.
Storage at the top. The top matters most for storage, because
it holds a fraction (B −1)/B of the capacity: half at B = 2
and seven eighths at B = 8. Its published table must stay
queryable while the replacement is built, so the top needs
at least two regions of T buckets. MICRORAMA needs no
more, because the butterfly network rebuilds a table in place:
preparation routes the published table into the second region,
and the sweep then inserts the carry there.
Storage bound. The three regions of a non-top level hold
3 −1/B times its full table, and the two regions of the top
hold twice its table. With half-full hash tables, MICRORAMA
with B = 2, 4, and 8 therefore uses about 9×, 5.8×, and
4.8× the space of the raw data, respectively. These numbers
